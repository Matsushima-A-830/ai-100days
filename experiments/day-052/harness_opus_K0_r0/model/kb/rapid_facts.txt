# Rapid facts across topics
Water molecules stick together, which is called cohesion and causes surface tension.
Surface tension lets insects such as water striders walk on water and lets a paper clip float.
Water moves up thin tubes and through paper towels by capillary action.
Water is a polar molecule, which is why it dissolves many substances.
Water expands about 9 percent when it freezes.
The mass of a substance stays the same when it changes state, such as melting or freezing.
When water boils, bubbles of water vapor form inside the liquid.
Steam is invisible water vapor; the white cloud above a kettle is tiny droplets of condensed water.
Evaporation causes cooling because the fastest-moving particles escape from the liquid.
Gases expand to fill any container, and their particles move in all directions.
Liquids flow and take the shape of their containers.
Solids keep their shape because their particles are held tightly in place.
Heating a solid makes its particles vibrate faster until it melts.
Cooling a liquid slows its particles until they lock into place and it freezes.
The volume of an irregular object such as a rock can be measured by how much water it displaces.
Tropisms are plant growth responses: phototropism toward light, gravitropism to gravity, thigmotropism to touch and hydrotropism toward water.
Vines wrapping around a pole show thigmotropism, a response to touch.
Roots growing toward water show hydrotropism.
Animals with an endoskeleton have bones inside their bodies; animals with an exoskeleton have a hard covering outside.
Vertebrates have an internal skeleton with a backbone.
Warm-blooded animals such as birds and mammals use energy from food to keep a steady body temperature.
Cold-blooded animals such as reptiles, amphibians and fish need less food than warm-blooded animals.
Mitosis produces new cells for growth and repair of the body.
Skin cells are replaced constantly by cell division.
A cut heals as new skin cells are produced by mitosis.
The nucleus contains chromosomes, which contain DNA.
Photosynthesis stores energy from sunlight in glucose; cellular respiration releases that energy.
Photosynthesis and cellular respiration are opposite processes.
Earth's layers increase in density from the crust to the core.

Uneven heating of Earth's surface by the Sun causes convection currents in the atmosphere, which make wind.

Halite can be identified by its salty taste and cubic crystals.

Coal forms in stages from peat to lignite to bituminous coal to anthracite as heat and pressure increase.
Mass movement is the downhill movement of rock and soil due to gravity, such as landslides, mudflows and creep.
Creep is the very slow downhill movement of soil that can tilt fences and trees.
Mudslides happen when heavy rain soaks soil on steep slopes.
Avalanches are masses of snow sliding down a mountain.
Rockfalls happen when rocks break off steep cliffs.

A stream's speed depends on the slope of the land and the amount of water.
Faster-moving water can carry larger sediments.
When water slows, the largest and heaviest sediments settle first.
In a jar of shaken soil and water, the gravel settles first, then sand, then silt, and clay settles last.
Sorting of sediments by size happens when moving water slows down.
Wind can only carry small particles such as sand and dust.
Glaciers can carry sediments of all sizes, from clay to huge boulders, and leave them unsorted.
A moraine is a ridge of rocks and soil deposited by a glacier.
Glacial striations are scratches on bedrock made by rocks dragged by glaciers.
Kettle lakes form when blocks of glacial ice melt in depressions.
Sea arches, sea stacks and sea caves are formed by wave erosion.
Jetties and seawalls are built to reduce beach erosion.
Sand dunes move in the direction the wind blows.
Plants with deep roots on dunes help hold the sand in place.
Erosion by water is fastest on steep slopes with no plants after heavy rain.
The amount of erosion depends on the slope, the amount of water, the type of soil and plant cover.
Soil erosion removes fertile topsoil that farmers need.
The Dust Bowl of the 1930s happened when drought and poor farming let wind blow away topsoil on the Great Plains.
Contour plowing follows the curves of the land to slow runoff and reduce erosion.
Terracing creates flat steps on hillsides to reduce runoff and erosion.
Windbreaks are rows of trees that slow wind and reduce erosion.
Cover crops protect soil from erosion when fields are not being used.
No-till farming leaves crop remains on fields to protect the soil.
Weathering happens faster in places with lots of rain and temperature changes.
Rocks in deserts are weathered mostly by wind and temperature changes.
Hard rocks such as granite weather more slowly than soft rocks such as limestone or sandstone.
Gravestones made of marble weather faster than those made of granite.
Old gravestones become hard to read because of chemical weathering by acid rain.
Statues and buildings made of limestone and marble are damaged by acid rain.
The Statue of Liberty turned green because the copper reacted with air and water.
Burning sulfur-rich coal produces sulfur dioxide, which causes acid rain.
Lakes harmed by acid rain can be treated by adding lime, a base.
Fish and plants in lakes die when the water becomes too acidic.
Scientists measure the pH of rain and lakes to monitor acid rain.
The water in most lakes and rivers is fresh water with very little salt.
Brackish water is a mix of fresh and salt water, found in estuaries.
The Dead Sea and Great Salt Lake are very salty lakes.
People cannot drink salt water because it dehydrates the body.
About 71 percent of Earth's surface is covered by water.
Less than 1 percent of Earth's water is available fresh water for people to use.
Most fresh water on Earth is frozen in glaciers and ice caps, especially in Antarctica and Greenland.

Wells must be deep enough to reach the water table.

Reservoirs are artificial lakes made by dams to store water.
Water towers use gravity to supply water pressure to homes.
Drinking water is cleaned by filtering, settling and adding chlorine.
Boiling water kills most germs.
A water filter removes particles but may not remove dissolved salts.
Distillation can remove salt from water by boiling and condensing the water vapor.

Most of the energy used in the United States comes from fossil fuels: oil, natural gas and coal.

The ovary of a flower becomes the fruit, and the ovules become seeds.
