# Inventions, scientists, technology, society
Galileo Galilei improved the telescope and observed the moons of Jupiter, sunspots and the phases of Venus.
Isaac Newton described the laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation.
Albert Einstein developed the theory of relativity and the equation E equals mc squared, relating mass and energy.
Marie Curie studied radioactivity and discovered the elements polonium and radium.
Charles Darwin proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection.
Gregor Mendel discovered the basic laws of heredity by experimenting with pea plants.
Louis Pasteur showed that microorganisms cause disease and spoil food, and developed pasteurization and vaccines.
Thomas Edison invented a practical light bulb and the phonograph.
Alexander Graham Bell invented the telephone.
The Wright brothers built and flew the first successful airplane in 1903.
Benjamin Franklin experimented with lightning and electricity using a kite and invented the lightning rod.
Johannes Gutenberg invented the printing press with movable type, which spread information widely.
James Watt improved the steam engine, which powered the Industrial Revolution.
Samuel Morse invented the telegraph and Morse code.
Henry Ford used the assembly line to mass-produce cars.
Eli Whitney invented the cotton gin, which separated cotton fibers from seeds.
Guglielmo Marconi developed the radio.
Nikola Tesla developed alternating current electricity and motors.
Michael Faraday discovered that moving a magnet near a wire produces electric current.
Antoine Lavoisier showed that mass is conserved in chemical reactions.
Dmitri Mendeleev created the periodic table of the elements.
John Dalton proposed that all matter is made of atoms.
Ernest Rutherford discovered that atoms have a small dense nucleus.
Nicolaus Copernicus proposed that the Sun is at the center of the solar system.
Johannes Kepler found that planets orbit the Sun in ellipses.
Edwin Hubble discovered that there are other galaxies and that the universe is expanding.
Rachel Carson wrote Silent Spring about the dangers of pesticides such as DDT.
Jane Goodall studied chimpanzees in the wild.
George Washington Carver found many uses for peanuts and promoted crop rotation.
Jonas Salk developed a vaccine against polio.
Anton van Leeuwenhoek made simple microscopes and was the first to see bacteria.
Robert Hooke named cells after looking at cork under a microscope.
Carl Linnaeus developed the system of classifying organisms with two-part scientific names.
Alfred Wegener proposed the theory of continental drift.
James Hutton is called the father of geology; he proposed that Earth's surface changed slowly over long periods.
Neil Armstrong was the first person to walk on the Moon in 1969 during the Apollo 11 mission.
Sputnik was the first artificial satellite, launched in 1957.
The Hubble Space Telescope orbits Earth and takes clear pictures of distant galaxies.
Rovers such as Curiosity and Perseverance explore the surface of Mars.
The invention of the telescope allowed people to see objects in space more clearly.
The invention of the microscope allowed scientists to discover cells and germs.
The invention of the telephone, radio and internet improved communication over long distances.
The invention of the steam engine and the railroad made transportation faster.
The invention of refrigeration allowed food to be stored longer and shipped farther.
The light bulb allowed people to work and read after dark.
The printing press made books cheaper and spread knowledge.
Vaccines and antibiotics greatly reduced deaths from infectious diseases.
Computers process and store information.
The internet connects computers around the world.
GPS uses satellites to determine location.
Robots are machines that can do tasks automatically, such as building cars or exploring other planets.
Biotechnology uses living things to make products, such as insulin made by bacteria.
Genetic engineering can produce crops that resist insects or drought.
Plastics are useful because they are light, strong, cheap and waterproof, but they cause pollution.
Kevlar is a strong fiber used in bulletproof vests.
Velcro was invented by copying the way burrs stick to animal fur.
Many inventions copy designs found in nature, called biomimicry.
Bridges are built with strong materials such as steel and concrete.
Concrete is made from cement, sand, gravel and water.
Engineers test materials for strength, flexibility, durability and cost.
A cell phone sends and receives radio waves.
A thermostat controls the temperature of a room by turning heating or cooling on and off.
A refrigerator moves heat from inside to outside to keep food cold.
Air conditioners move heat from inside a building to the outside.
A microwave oven uses microwaves to heat water molecules in food.
A solar oven uses reflective surfaces to focus sunlight and heat food.
A water wheel uses flowing water to turn machinery.
Windmills were used to grind grain and pump water.
Irrigation brings water to crops in dry areas.
Tractors and farm machines allow fewer farmers to grow more food.
Fertilizers and pesticides increase crop yields but can pollute water.
Greenhouses allow plants to grow in winter.
Levees and dams help control flooding.
Seismographs help scientists detect earthquakes, and warning systems can alert people.
Weather satellites and radar help forecasters warn people about hurricanes and tornadoes.
Lightning rods protect buildings from lightning.
Smoke detectors warn people about fires.
Seat belts and airbags protect people in car crashes.
Helmets protect the head from injury when riding bikes.
Recycling centers separate materials such as paper, plastic, glass and metal.
A water filter removes particles from water.
Chlorine is added to drinking water to kill bacteria.
Sewage treatment protects rivers and lakes from pollution.
Hybrid cars use both gasoline engines and electric motors.
LED light bulbs use much less energy than incandescent bulbs and last longer.
Solar panels on roofs let homes produce their own electricity.
Batteries store energy for later use, such as in phones and electric cars.
Improvements in technology allow scientists to collect more accurate data.
New technology can lead to new scientific discoveries, and new discoveries can lead to new technology.
Scientists from many countries work together on projects such as the International Space Station.
Ethics in science means considering whether research is right and safe for people, animals and the environment.
