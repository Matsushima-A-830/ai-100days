# Investigations: design, data, conclusions (phrasings common in test questions)
The best way to make an investigation more reliable is to repeat it several times and compare the results.
Conducting more trials and testing more samples makes the results of an investigation more valid.
Using a larger number of plants, animals or samples in an experiment gives more reliable results.
To test one variable, all other conditions must be kept the same so the comparison is fair.
A student testing how sunlight affects plant growth should give all plants the same water, soil and temperature but different amounts of light.
A student testing how water affects plant growth should vary the amount of water and keep light, soil and plant type the same.
A student testing whether fertilizer helps plants grow should have one group with fertilizer and one control group without fertilizer.
When testing which paper towel absorbs the most water, use the same size towel and the same amount of water for each brand.
When testing how temperature affects how fast sugar dissolves, use the same amount of sugar and water and change only the water temperature.
When testing which ball bounces highest, drop each ball from the same height onto the same surface.
When testing how the height of a ramp affects the speed of a car, use the same car and change only the ramp height.
When testing which insulator keeps water hot longest, start with the same amount of water at the same temperature in each container.
The variable that is measured, such as plant height, bounce height or time, is the dependent variable.
The variable a student purposely changes, such as drop height, amount of water or temperature, is the independent variable.
Results should be recorded in a data table during the investigation.
Measurements should be taken with the correct tool and recorded with units.
A scientist should report results honestly even if they do not support the hypothesis.
If an experiment's results do not match the prediction, the scientist should check the procedure, repeat the experiment, or revise the hypothesis.
A conclusion should be based on the data collected in the investigation.
An investigation with only one trial or one sample may give results that are due to chance.
Scientists compare their results with results from other scientists to check their accuracy.
Scientists communicate results through reports, journals, graphs, presentations and the internet.
A good scientific question can be tested by an experiment and measured.
Which one is better or prettier is an opinion and cannot be tested scientifically.
Asking how the amount of light affects plant growth is a testable scientific question.
A prediction based on a pattern in data is called an inference or a hypothesis.
Data in a table can be shown in a graph to make patterns easier to see.
Line graphs are best for showing changes over time, such as temperature during a day or plant growth over weeks.
Bar graphs are best for comparing different groups, such as the number of each kind of bird seen.
Circle graphs show percentages of a whole, such as the parts of Earth's atmosphere.
Scatter plots show the relationship between two variables.
An outlier is a data point that is very different from the others and may be due to an error.
The mean is the average of a set of numbers; the range is the difference between the largest and smallest values.
Precise measurements use tools with smaller units, such as millimeters instead of centimeters.
Observing with the senses, measuring, classifying, inferring, predicting and communicating are science process skills.
Classifying means sorting objects into groups based on their properties.
Scientists use models when the real object is too large, too small, too far away or too dangerous.
A globe is a model of Earth; a map is a flat model of an area.
A diagram of the water cycle is a model that shows how water moves.
A computer simulation can model events that take a very long time, such as climate change or plate movement.
Models can be used to make predictions and explain observations.
Evidence from many different investigations makes a scientific explanation stronger.
Scientific ideas change when new evidence is found, for example new technology like telescopes and microscopes.
Scientists cannot prove a hypothesis absolutely true; they can only support it with evidence.
Sharing data allows other scientists to repeat the investigation and verify the results.
Field guides help identify plants and animals.
A science journal or notebook records observations, data, drawings and questions.
Safety equipment must be used even in a short experiment.
Scientists wear gloves and goggles when handling chemicals.
When observing animals in an experiment, scientists must treat them humanely and return them to their habitat.
Students should never taste or smell unknown substances directly.
Electric equipment should be kept away from water during investigations.
Scientists measure the same thing several times and take the average to reduce errors.
Reliable data come from careful measurements repeated many times.
Valid conclusions come from controlled experiments with only one variable changed.
An experiment needs a control so the results can be compared to normal conditions.
When an experiment has more than one variable changed, the results cannot be explained clearly.
Using different kinds of plants in different groups would add another variable.
The hypothesis should be testable and based on observations.
After drawing a conclusion, scientists often ask new questions and plan new investigations.
Good observations are detailed and accurate and include measurements.
Observations describe what is seen; inferences explain what was seen.
The sentence "the leaf is green and 5 centimeters long" is an observation.
The sentence "the plant died because it did not get enough water" is an inference.
Temperature data collected at the same time each day can be compared fairly.
To study the effect of a variable on a population, scientists may compare an area with the variable to an area without it.
Long-term studies show patterns over many years, such as climate trends and population changes.
Tools such as probes, sensors and computers help collect data automatically.
Scientists use satellite images to observe changes in forests, ice and cities over time.
A hand lens is used to look at details of rocks, insects and leaves.
Engineers build and test prototypes, find problems and redesign them.
The best design solution meets the criteria within the constraints.
Testing a design many times shows whether it works reliably.
