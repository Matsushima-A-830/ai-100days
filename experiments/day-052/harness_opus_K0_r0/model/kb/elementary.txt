# Elementary-level facts in simple words
Plants need sunlight, water, air and soil to grow.
Animals need food, water, air and shelter to live.
People need food, water, air, shelter and clothing.
Living things grow, change, and make new living things like themselves.
Baby animals look like their parents, and seedlings grow into plants like their parents.
Young animals often look like their parents but are smaller.
A puppy grows into a dog; a kitten grows into a cat; a calf grows into a cow; a chick grows into a chicken; a foal grows into a horse.
A duckling grows into a duck; a tadpole grows into a frog; a caterpillar grows into a butterfly; a cub grows into a bear.
Most plants grow from seeds.
A seed grows into a seedling and then into an adult plant that makes flowers and new seeds.
An acorn grows into an oak tree.
Plants make their own food from sunlight, but animals must eat food.
Some animals eat plants, some eat other animals, and some eat both.
Animals use their senses to find food and stay safe.
Animals move in different ways: they walk, run, hop, swim, fly, crawl and slither.
Fish swim with fins; birds fly with wings; snakes slither on their bellies; kangaroos hop.
Animals live in habitats such as forests, deserts, oceans, ponds, grasslands and the arctic.
A pond is a habitat for frogs, fish, ducks, turtles and dragonflies.
A desert is a hot, dry habitat for cactus, lizards, snakes and camels.
The ocean is a salty water habitat for whales, sharks, fish, octopuses and seaweed.
A forest is a habitat with many trees for deer, owls, squirrels, bears and woodpeckers.
The arctic is a cold, snowy habitat for polar bears, seals, walruses and arctic foxes.
Animals build homes: birds build nests, beavers build lodges, bees build hives, spiders spin webs, rabbits dig burrows.
Some animals sleep through the winter, and some move to warmer places.
In fall, leaves change color and fall from many trees; animals gather food.
In winter, it is cold, days are short, some animals hibernate and some birds fly south.
In spring, plants begin to grow, flowers bloom, and many animals have babies.
In summer, it is hot, days are long, and plants grow quickly.
The four seasons are spring, summer, fall or autumn, and winter.
The seasons repeat in a pattern every year.
Day and night repeat every 24 hours.
We can see the Sun during the day and the Moon and stars at night.
Sometimes the Moon can be seen during the day.
The Sun warms the land, water and air.
Shade is cooler than sunny places because the Sun's light is blocked.
Things in sunlight get warmer than things in shade.
Clouds are made of tiny drops of water.
Rain, snow, sleet and hail fall from clouds.
Wind is moving air.
We use a thermometer to tell how hot or cold it is.
Weather can be sunny, cloudy, rainy, snowy, windy, hot or cold.
People wear coats in cold weather and light clothes in hot weather.
Weather changes from day to day and from season to season.
Rocks come in different sizes, shapes, colors and textures.
Rocks can be sorted by size, color, shape, texture and hardness.
Soil is made of tiny bits of rock, dead plants and animals, water and air.
Sand feels gritty, clay feels sticky when wet, and silt feels smooth.
Water can be a solid, a liquid or a gas.
Ice is solid water; steam is water as a gas.
Ice melts when it gets warm and water freezes when it gets very cold.
Water can change from a liquid to a gas when it is heated.
Objects can be described by their color, size, shape, weight and texture.
Some objects float on water and some objects sink.
A cork, a leaf, a plastic toy and wood float; a rock, a coin, a key and a nail sink.
Magnets pick up things made of iron such as nails and paper clips.
Magnets do not pick up paper, wood, plastic or glass.
Pushes and pulls make things move, stop or change direction.
A harder push makes an object move faster and farther.
Heavier objects need a bigger push or pull to move.
Things fall down to the ground because of gravity.
Balls roll faster down steeper ramps.
Smooth surfaces let things slide easily; rough surfaces slow them down.
Sound is made when things vibrate, or move back and forth quickly.
We hear sounds with our ears.
Loud sounds and soft sounds; high sounds and low sounds.
A drum makes sound when it is hit and its top vibrates.
Light lets us see things.
Light comes from the Sun, lamps, flashlights, fire and candles.
Shadows form when something blocks light.
Shadows change size and direction during the day as the Sun moves across the sky.
Mirrors reflect light.
We see colors when light reflects off objects.
Electricity powers lights, televisions, computers and many machines.
Batteries store energy for flashlights, toys and phones.
Never put objects in electrical outlets.
Electricity can be dangerous near water.
We use energy to cook, heat our homes, run cars and power machines.
Food gives our bodies energy.
We should save energy by turning off lights and water when not in use.
People can reduce, reuse and recycle to help Earth.
Littering harms animals and makes places dirty.
Trees give us wood, paper, fruit, shade, oxygen and homes for animals.
Plants give us food, cotton for clothes, wood and medicine.
Animals give us food, wool, leather and help with work.
Cows give milk; chickens give eggs; sheep give wool; bees make honey.
We breathe with our lungs and our heart pumps blood.
Our bones hold us up and protect our insides; our muscles help us move.
Our five senses help us learn about the world: see, hear, smell, taste and touch.
We see with our eyes, hear with our ears, smell with our nose, taste with our tongue and touch with our skin.
Eating healthy food, drinking water, exercising and sleeping keep us healthy.
Washing hands keeps germs away.
Teeth should be brushed twice a day.
Scientists ask questions, observe, measure, test ideas and share what they learn.
A hand lens helps us see small things up close.
A ruler measures how long something is.
A scale or balance measures how heavy something is.
A measuring cup measures how much liquid there is.
A clock or timer measures time.
Goggles protect our eyes during experiments.
