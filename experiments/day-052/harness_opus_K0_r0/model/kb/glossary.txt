# Glossary: one term and its definition per line
Absorption: the taking in of light, heat, sound or a substance by a material.
Acceleration: the rate at which velocity changes; speeding up, slowing down or changing direction.
Acid rain: rain made acidic by sulfur and nitrogen pollution from burning fossil fuels.
Adaptation: an inherited trait or behavior that helps an organism survive in its environment.
Air mass: a large body of air with the same temperature and humidity.
Air pressure: the force of air pressing down on an area.
Allele: one of the different forms of a gene.
Altitude: the height above sea level.
Amplitude: the height of a wave from its rest position; larger amplitude means more energy and louder sound.
Anemometer: an instrument that measures wind speed.
Aquifer: an underground layer of rock that stores groundwater.
Asexual reproduction: reproduction with one parent that produces identical offspring.
Asteroid: a rocky object orbiting the Sun, mostly between Mars and Jupiter.
Atmosphere: the layer of gases surrounding Earth.
Atom: the smallest unit of an element.
Axis: an imaginary line through the North and South Poles around which Earth spins.
Bacteria: single-celled microorganisms without a nucleus.
Barometer: an instrument that measures air pressure.
Biodiversity: the variety of different species living in an area.
Biome: a large area with a particular climate and plants and animals, such as a desert or rain forest.
Biosphere: the part of Earth where life exists.
Boiling point: the temperature at which a liquid changes to a gas; water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.
Buoyancy: the upward force of a liquid or gas on an object.
Camouflage: coloring or patterns that help an animal blend into its surroundings.
Carnivore: an animal that eats only other animals.
Carrying capacity: the largest population an environment can support.
Cell: the smallest unit of life.
Cell membrane: the thin layer that surrounds a cell and controls what enters and leaves.
Cell wall: the stiff outer layer that supports a plant cell.
Chemical change: a change that produces a new substance.
Chemical energy: energy stored in the bonds of substances such as food, fuel and batteries.
Chlorophyll: green pigment in plants that captures light energy.
Chloroplast: the organelle where photosynthesis happens.
Chromosome: a structure in the nucleus made of DNA that carries genes.
Circuit: a closed path through which electricity flows.
Climate: the average weather of a place over many years.
Comet: a ball of ice and dust orbiting the Sun with a glowing tail.
Community: all the populations living together in an area.
Compound: a substance made of two or more elements chemically joined.
Condensation: the change from a gas to a liquid.
Conduction: transfer of heat by direct contact.
Conductor: a material that lets heat or electricity flow easily.
Conservation: saving and protecting natural resources.
Constellation: a group of stars forming a pattern in the sky.
Consumer: an organism that eats other organisms for energy.
Continental drift: the slow movement of continents over Earth's surface.
Control: the part of an experiment kept unchanged for comparison.
Convection: transfer of heat by the movement of liquids or gases.
Core: the center of Earth, made of iron and nickel.
Crust: Earth's thin, solid outer layer.
Cytoplasm: the gel-like material inside a cell.
Decomposer: an organism that breaks down dead plants and animals.
Density: mass per unit volume.
Deposition: the dropping of sediment by wind, water or ice.
Dependent variable: the factor measured in an experiment that responds to changes.
Dew point: the temperature at which water vapor condenses into liquid.
Diffusion: movement of particles from where they are crowded to where they are less crowded.
Digestion: breaking down food into nutrients the body can use.
DNA: the molecule that carries genetic instructions.
Dominant trait: a trait that shows up when at least one dominant allele is present.
Dormant: in a resting state with little activity, such as a seed or a volcano that may erupt again.
Echo: a reflected sound.
Eclipse: when one object in space blocks the light reaching another.
Ecosystem: living and nonliving things interacting in an area.
Electromagnet: a magnet made by electric current flowing through a wire coiled around iron.
Electron: a negatively charged particle that orbits the nucleus of an atom.
Element: a substance made of only one type of atom.
Embryo: an organism in its earliest stage of development, such as the baby plant inside a seed.
Endangered: a species at risk of becoming extinct.
Energy: the ability to do work or cause change.
Energy pyramid: a diagram showing energy decreasing at each level of a food chain.
Epicenter: the point on Earth's surface above where an earthquake starts.
Equator: an imaginary line around the middle of Earth halfway between the poles.
Erosion: the carrying away of weathered rock and soil by water, wind, ice or gravity.
Evaporation: the change from a liquid to a gas.
Evolution: change in species over long periods of time.
Exoskeleton: a hard outer covering of an arthropod.
Extinct: a species that no longer has any living members.
Fault: a break in Earth's crust where rocks move.
Fertilization: the joining of a male sex cell and a female sex cell.
Food chain: the path of energy as one organism eats another.
Food web: overlapping food chains in an ecosystem.
Force: a push or a pull.
Fossil: remains or traces of an organism that lived long ago.
Fossil fuel: coal, oil or natural gas formed from ancient organisms.
Frequency: the number of waves passing a point per second; higher frequency gives higher pitch.
Friction: a force that resists motion between surfaces.
Front: the boundary between two air masses.
Galaxy: a huge system of stars, gas and dust.
Gene: a segment of DNA that controls a trait.
Germination: the sprouting of a seed.
Glacier: a large mass of moving ice.
Gravity: a force of attraction between objects with mass.
Greenhouse effect: warming of Earth as gases trap heat.
Groundwater: water stored underground in soil and rock.
Habitat: the place where an organism lives.
Hardness: how easily a mineral can be scratched.
Herbivore: an animal that eats only plants.
Heredity: the passing of traits from parents to offspring.
Hibernation: a deep sleep some animals go into during winter.
Humidity: the amount of water vapor in the air.
Humus: decayed plant and animal matter in soil.
Hypothesis: a testable prediction.
Igneous rock: rock formed from cooled magma or lava.
Independent variable: the factor that is changed in an experiment.
Inertia: the tendency of an object to resist a change in motion.
Inference: a conclusion based on observations.
Inherited trait: a characteristic passed from parents to offspring.
Instinct: a behavior an animal is born knowing.
Insulator: a material that does not let heat or electricity pass through easily.
Invertebrate: an animal without a backbone.
Kinetic energy: energy of motion.
Larva: the young, wormlike stage of an insect after hatching.
Lava: magma that has reached Earth's surface.
Learned behavior: a behavior an animal gets from experience.
Lever: a bar that pivots on a fulcrum.
Life cycle: the stages of growth and change in an organism's life.
Light-year: the distance light travels in a year.
Luster: the way a mineral's surface reflects light.
Magma: melted rock beneath Earth's surface.
Magnetic field: the area around a magnet where magnetic force acts.
Mantle: the thick layer of Earth between the crust and the core.
Mass: the amount of matter in an object.
Matter: anything that has mass and takes up space.
Melting point: the temperature at which a solid becomes a liquid.
Metamorphic rock: rock changed by heat and pressure.
Metamorphosis: a series of changes in body form during an animal's life cycle.
Meteor: a streak of light from a space rock burning in the atmosphere.
Meteorite: a space rock that lands on Earth.
Microorganism: a living thing too small to see without a microscope.
Migration: seasonal movement of animals from one place to another.
Mimicry: when one organism looks like another to protect itself.
Mineral: a naturally formed, nonliving solid with a crystal structure.
Mitochondria: organelles that release energy from food.
Mixture: two or more substances combined but not chemically joined.
Molecule: two or more atoms bonded together.
Moon phase: the shape of the lit part of the Moon seen from Earth.
Motion: a change in position.
Mutation: a change in a gene or DNA.
Mutualism: a relationship in which both organisms benefit.
Natural resource: a material from nature used by people.
Natural selection: the process by which organisms best suited to their environment survive and reproduce.
Nebula: a cloud of gas and dust in space where stars form.
Neutron: a particle with no charge in the nucleus of an atom.
Niche: the role of an organism in its ecosystem.
Nonrenewable resource: a resource that cannot be replaced in a short time.
Nucleus: the control center of a cell; also the center of an atom with protons and neutrons.
Nutrient: a substance an organism needs to live and grow.
Omnivore: an animal that eats both plants and animals.
Opaque: blocking all light.
Orbit: the curved path of one object around another in space.
Organ: a group of tissues that work together.
Organism: any living thing.
Ozone layer: a layer of the atmosphere that absorbs ultraviolet rays.
Parasite: an organism that lives on or in a host and harms it.
Photosynthesis: the process by which plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make sugar and oxygen.
Physical change: a change in size, shape or state that does not form a new substance.
Pitch: how high or low a sound is.
Pollination: moving pollen from stamen to pistil.
Pollution: harmful substances in the environment.
Population: all members of one species in an area.
Potential energy: stored energy.
Precipitation: water falling from clouds as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
Predator: an animal that hunts other animals.
Prey: an animal that is hunted and eaten.
Prism: a clear object that separates white light into colors.
Producer: an organism that makes its own food.
Proton: a positively charged particle in the nucleus.
Pupa: the resting stage between larva and adult in complete metamorphosis.
Radiation: transfer of energy by waves through space.
Recessive trait: a trait that appears only when two recessive alleles are present.
Recycle: to process used materials to make new products.
Reflection: bouncing of light, sound or heat off a surface.
Refraction: bending of light as it passes from one material to another.
Renewable resource: a resource that can be replaced naturally.
Reproduction: the process of making offspring.
Respiration: the process cells use to release energy from food; also breathing.
Revolution: one complete trip of an object around another.
Rock cycle: the process by which rocks change from one type to another.
Rotation: spinning on an axis.
Runoff: water that flows over land into streams and rivers.
Satellite: an object that orbits a planet.
Sediment: bits of rock, soil and organic material carried by water, wind or ice.
Sedimentary rock: rock formed from layers of sediment pressed together.
Seismograph: an instrument that records earthquake waves.
Sexual reproduction: reproduction involving two parents.
Simple machine: a device that makes work easier, such as a lever, pulley or ramp.
Soil: loose material of weathered rock, humus, air and water.
Solar system: the Sun and all objects that orbit it.
Solubility: how much of a substance can dissolve.
Solution: a mixture in which one substance dissolves evenly in another.
Species: a group of similar organisms that can reproduce together.
Speed: distance traveled per unit of time.
Static electricity: buildup of electric charge on an object.
Stomata: tiny openings in leaves for gas exchange.
Sublimation: change from a solid directly to a gas.
Symbiosis: a close relationship between two species.
Temperature: a measure of how hot or cold something is.
Tide: the regular rise and fall of ocean water caused by the Moon's gravity.
Tissue: a group of similar cells doing the same job.
Topsoil: the upper layer of soil rich in humus.
Trait: a characteristic of an organism.
Translucent: letting some light pass through.
Transparent: letting all light pass through.
Transpiration: water loss through the leaves of plants.
Tropism: growth of a plant toward or away from a stimulus.
Vacuole: a storage sac in a cell.
Variable: a factor that can change in an experiment.
Vertebrate: an animal with a backbone.
Vibration: a rapid back and forth movement.
Volcano: an opening in the crust where magma erupts.
Volume: the amount of space an object occupies; also loudness of sound.
Water cycle: the continuous movement of water on, above and below Earth's surface.
Wavelength: the distance between two crests of a wave.
Weather: the condition of the atmosphere at a given time and place.
Weathering: the breaking down of rock into smaller pieces.
Weight: the force of gravity on an object.
Work: force applied over a distance.
Diurnal: active during the day; most birds, butterflies and humans are diurnal.
Nocturnal: active at night; owls, bats and raccoons are nocturnal.
Radiant energy: energy carried by electromagnetic waves, such as light and heat from the Sun.
Solar energy is radiant energy from the Sun.
Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas.
