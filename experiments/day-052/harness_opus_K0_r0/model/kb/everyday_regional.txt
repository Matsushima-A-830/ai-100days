# Everyday science, regional facts, comparisons
An atom is smaller than a molecule, a molecule is smaller than a cell, and a cell is smaller than a tissue.
From smallest to largest: atom, molecule, cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism, population, community, ecosystem, biosphere.
Electrons are much smaller than protons and neutrons.
Bacteria are much smaller than plant and animal cells, and viruses are smaller than bacteria.
The Sun is much larger than Earth, and Earth is larger than the Moon.
Jupiter is the largest planet, and Mercury is the smallest planet.
Stars are much farther away than the planets.
The Moon is the closest large object in space to Earth.
A galaxy is larger than a solar system, which is larger than a planet.
Mount Everest is the highest mountain on Earth.
The Nile is one of the longest rivers in the world, and the Amazon carries the most water.
The Mississippi River is the largest river system in North America; it deposits sediment in a delta in the Gulf of Mexico.
The Gulf of Mexico has warm water where hurricanes often form and strike Texas, Louisiana and Florida.
Barrier islands along the Texas and Atlantic coasts protect the mainland from storm waves.
Texas has many ecosystems, including deserts in the west, grasslands and prairies, pine forests in the east, and coastal wetlands.
The Gulf Coast of Texas has wetlands, marshes and estuaries that are nurseries for fish and shrimp.
The Edwards Aquifer supplies groundwater to much of central Texas.
Oil and natural gas are important natural resources in Texas.
New York State was covered by glaciers during the last ice age, which carved the Finger Lakes and left boulders and moraines.
The Adirondack Mountains in New York are made of very old metamorphic and igneous rock.
Niagara Falls is slowly moving upstream because erosion wears away the rock under the falls.
Long Island was formed by glacial moraines.
The Great Lakes were carved by glaciers and hold a large part of the world's fresh surface water.
Florida has a warm, humid climate with wetlands such as the Everglades.
The Everglades is a large wetland in Florida with alligators, wading birds and sawgrass.
Alaska has tundra, glaciers, and long summer days and long winter nights because it is far north.
In Alaska in winter the Sun is low in the sky and there are few hours of daylight.
Hawaii's islands formed from volcanoes over a hot spot.
California has many earthquakes because of the San Andreas Fault.
Yellowstone National Park sits over a hot spot with geysers and hot springs.
The Rocky Mountains are young, tall mountains in the western United States.
The Appalachian Mountains are old mountains in the eastern United States that have been worn down by erosion.
The Great Plains are flat grasslands in the middle of the United States used for farming wheat and corn.
The Sonoran and Mojave Deserts are hot deserts in the southwestern United States.
Death Valley is one of the hottest and driest places in North America.
Virginia has a temperate climate with four seasons, mountains in the west and the Chesapeake Bay in the east.
The Chesapeake Bay is an estuary where fresh water from rivers mixes with salt water from the Atlantic Ocean.
Pollution from farms and cities flows into the Chesapeake Bay and harms oysters, crabs and fish.
The Atlantic Ocean lies to the east of North America; the Pacific Ocean lies to the west.
Hurricane season in the Atlantic is from June to November.
Tornadoes are most common in the Great Plains in spring.
Snow is most common in northern states in winter.
In the Northern Hemisphere, summer begins in June and winter begins in December.
In the Southern Hemisphere, such as Australia, summer is in December and January.
Days are longer in summer and shorter in winter.
In the United States the Sun is always in the southern part of the sky at noon.
A thermometer reading of 30 degrees Celsius is a hot day; 0 degrees Celsius is freezing; 20 degrees Celsius is room temperature.
Normal room temperature is about 20 to 25 degrees Celsius.
Water boils at a lower temperature at high altitudes such as on mountains.
Cooking food changes it chemically, making it easier to digest and killing bacteria.
Refrigerators slow the growth of microorganisms that spoil food.
Freezing food stops bacteria from growing.
Milk is pasteurized by heating it to kill harmful bacteria.
Washing fruits and vegetables removes dirt and germs.
Soap removes oils and germs from hands and dishes.
Bleach kills germs.
Vinegar is an acid used in cooking and cleaning.
Baking soda is a base used in baking to make dough rise.
Salt is used to preserve food and to melt ice on roads.
Sugar dissolves in water to form a solution.
Ice cubes float in a drink because ice is less dense than water.
Steam from boiling water can cause burns because it releases heat when it condenses.
Glass windows let light in and keep wind out.
Curtains and blinds block sunlight to keep rooms cooler.
Planting trees around a house gives shade in summer and blocks wind in winter.
Light-colored roofs reflect sunlight and keep buildings cooler.
A car parked in the sun gets hot inside because sunlight passes through the windows and is absorbed, trapping heat like a greenhouse.
Turning off a light saves electricity.
Unplugging chargers and devices saves energy.
A refrigerator uses electricity to move heat out of the food compartment.
Electric stoves change electrical energy into heat; gas stoves burn natural gas.
A microwave changes electrical energy into microwaves that heat food.
A flashlight needs batteries, a bulb, and a switch to make a complete circuit.
Batteries contain chemicals and should be recycled, not thrown in the trash.
Cell phones use batteries that store chemical energy.
Bicycles change the chemical energy of the rider's muscles into mechanical energy of motion.
When a person runs, chemical energy from food changes into kinetic energy and heat.
A wind-up toy stores energy in a spring, which changes to kinetic energy.
Playground slides are inclined planes; seesaws are levers; swings are pendulums.
A swing goes highest when you push at the right time.
A soccer ball changes direction when it is kicked because a force acts on it.
A baseball bat transfers energy to the ball.
Rubber soles on shoes increase friction and prevent slipping.
Ice skates have thin blades that reduce friction on ice.
Wet floors are slippery because water reduces friction.
Car tires have treads to increase friction on wet roads.
Helmets, knee pads and seat belts reduce injuries.
Sunglasses protect eyes from bright light and ultraviolet rays.
Earmuffs protect ears from loud noise.
Gloves and hats keep the body warm by reducing heat loss.
Clothing made of wool or fleece traps air and keeps people warm.
Cotton clothing is cool in summer because it lets air pass and absorbs sweat.
Dogs, cats and other pets need food, water, shelter and care.
Gardens need sunlight, water, good soil and space for plants to grow.
Compost made from food scraps adds nutrients to garden soil.
Worms in a compost pile break down food scraps.
Bird feeders attract birds in winter when food is scarce.
Fish in an aquarium need clean water with oxygen; an air pump adds oxygen.
Aquarium plants produce oxygen for fish.
A terrarium needs light, water, soil and plants.
