# Plants: structure, photosynthesis, reproduction, life cycles, responses
Plants are producers because they make their own food by photosynthesis.
Photosynthesis is the process plants use to make food, sugar or glucose, from carbon dioxide and water using energy from sunlight.
During photosynthesis, plants take in carbon dioxide and water and give off oxygen.
Photosynthesis changes light energy into chemical energy stored in sugar.
Photosynthesis takes place in the chloroplasts, mostly in the leaves.
Chlorophyll is the green pigment in plants that absorbs sunlight for photosynthesis.
Plants are green because chlorophyll reflects green light.
Plants need sunlight, water, carbon dioxide, nutrients and space to grow.
Plants need light to make food; a plant kept in the dark will turn yellow and die.
The oxygen we breathe is produced by plants and algae during photosynthesis.
Plants use carbon dioxide from the air, and animals use the oxygen plants release.
Plants also carry out cellular respiration, using oxygen and sugar to release energy, day and night.
Plants store extra food as starch in roots, stems, seeds and fruits.
Potatoes are underground stems called tubers that store starch.
Carrots and beets are roots that store food.
The main parts of a plant are roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits and seeds.
Roots hold the plant in the soil and absorb water and minerals from the soil.
Root hairs increase the surface area of roots to absorb more water.
Roots anchor the plant and help prevent soil erosion.
Some roots store food for the plant.
The stem supports the plant and carries water and nutrients between the roots and leaves.
Xylem tubes carry water and minerals from the roots up to the leaves.
Phloem tubes carry food, sugar, from the leaves to other parts of the plant.
Leaves are the main organs where photosynthesis takes place.
Leaves are broad and flat to capture the most sunlight.
Stomata are tiny openings on leaves that let carbon dioxide in and oxygen and water vapor out.
Transpiration is the loss of water vapor through the stomata of leaves.
A waxy layer called the cuticle on leaves reduces water loss.
Flowers are the reproductive parts of flowering plants.
The flower makes seeds for reproduction.
Bright colored petals and sweet nectar attract pollinators such as bees, butterflies, birds and bats.
The stamen is the male part of a flower; it makes pollen in the anther.
The pistil is the female part of a flower; it includes the stigma, style and ovary.
Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the stamen to the pistil of a flower.
Pollen can be carried by insects, birds, bats, wind or water.
Bees pollinate flowers while they collect nectar and pollen for food.
Bees and flowers have a mutualistic relationship; both benefit.
Without pollinators like bees, many plants could not produce fruits and seeds.
Fertilization happens when pollen joins with an egg cell in the ovary, forming a seed.
After fertilization the ovary grows into a fruit that holds the seeds.
A fruit is the part of a flowering plant that contains seeds.
Apples, tomatoes, cucumbers, pumpkins and peppers are fruits because they contain seeds.
Fruits protect seeds and help scatter them.
Seed dispersal moves seeds away from the parent plant so new plants have room, light and water to grow.
Seeds are dispersed by wind, water, animals and by bursting pods.
Dandelion and maple seeds have parts like parachutes or wings that let the wind carry them.
Burrs and seeds with hooks stick to animal fur and are carried to new places.
Animals eat fruits and drop the seeds in their wastes far from the parent plant.
Coconuts can float on water to disperse their seeds to new islands.
Squirrels bury acorns and nuts, and some grow into new trees.
A seed contains a baby plant, or embryo, and stored food, protected by a seed coat.
The seed coat protects the embryo; the cotyledon stores food for the young plant.
Germination is when a seed begins to sprout and grow into a seedling.
Seeds need water, air or oxygen, and the right temperature to germinate.
Most seeds do not need light to germinate because they use their stored food until leaves grow.
The plant life cycle is seed, germination, seedling, adult plant, flower, pollination, fruit and seeds.
Flowering plants are called angiosperms; their seeds are inside fruits.
Conifers such as pine trees are gymnosperms that make seeds in cones rather than flowers.
Pine trees reproduce with cones and keep their needle leaves all year; they are evergreens.
Ferns and mosses reproduce with spores instead of seeds.
Mosses are small nonvascular plants with no true roots, stems or leaves; they grow in damp, shady places.
Vascular plants have tubes, xylem and phloem, to carry water and food; nonvascular plants such as mosses do not.
Some plants reproduce asexually from stems, leaves, roots, bulbs or runners.
Strawberry plants send out runners that grow into new plants.
Tulips and onions grow from bulbs.
New plants grown from cuttings are identical to the parent plant.
Deciduous trees lose their leaves in the fall; evergreen trees keep their leaves all year.
Leaves change color in autumn because chlorophyll breaks down, revealing yellow and orange pigments.
Trees lose their leaves in winter to save water and energy when there is less sunlight.
Plants grow toward light; this response is called phototropism.
A plant placed by a window will bend toward the light.
Roots grow down because of gravity, and stems grow up.
Some plants climb using tendrils that wrap around supports.
Plant growth is affected by light, water, temperature, soil nutrients and space.
Fertilizer provides nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium that help plants grow.
Too much fertilizer can harm plants and pollute water.
Plants grown without enough light become tall, thin, weak and pale.
Overwatering a plant can rot its roots.
Cactus plants survive in the desert with thick stems that store water, spines instead of leaves, a waxy coating, and wide shallow roots.
Spines on a cactus protect it from animals and reduce water loss.
Desert plants have small leaves or spines to reduce water loss.
Plants in rain forests often have large leaves to capture light in the shade and drip tips to shed water.
Water plants like water lilies have air spaces that help their leaves float.
Some plants have thorns to protect them from being eaten by animals.
Some plants produce poisons or bad tastes to keep animals from eating them.
Carnivorous plants such as the Venus flytrap trap and digest insects to get nitrogen in poor soil.
Tall trees in forests compete for sunlight.
Plants compete with each other for light, water, nutrients and space.
Weeds compete with crop plants for water, nutrients and sunlight.
Grass grows from its base, so it can survive grazing and mowing.
Tree rings show the age of a tree; one ring grows each year.
Wood is made of xylem cells from the trunk of a tree.
Rice, wheat and corn are grains, the seeds of grass plants, that feed much of the world.
Farmers rotate crops and plant legumes like beans and clover to add nitrogen to the soil.
Plants provide food, oxygen, wood, paper, cotton, medicines and shelter.
Paper is made from wood fibers from trees.
Cotton cloth is made from fibers of the cotton plant.
Plants remove carbon dioxide from the air.
Plants are the first link in most food chains.
Some plants are annuals that live one season, while perennials live for many years.
Bulbs, seeds and dormant buds help plants survive winter.
Dormancy is a period when a plant or seed stops growing to survive harsh conditions.
Selective breeding of plants produces crops with desirable traits such as larger fruit or resistance to disease.
Genetically engineered crops have genes added to give them traits such as resistance to insects.
A greenhouse lets sunlight in and traps heat so plants can grow in cold weather.
Hydroponics grows plants in nutrient-rich water instead of soil.
