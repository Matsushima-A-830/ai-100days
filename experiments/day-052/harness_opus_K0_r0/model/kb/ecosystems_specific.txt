# Specific ecosystems, food webs and organisms
In a desert ecosystem, cactus and shrubs are producers, insects, lizards and kangaroo rats are consumers, and snakes, hawks and coyotes are predators.
A desert food chain: cactus, insect, lizard, snake, hawk.
Desert animals such as camels, lizards and snakes are adapted to hot, dry conditions.
In a grassland ecosystem, grasses are producers, grasshoppers, prairie dogs and bison are herbivores, and hawks and coyotes are predators.
A grassland food chain: grass, grasshopper, mouse, snake, hawk.
Prairie dogs dig burrows that provide homes for other animals such as owls and snakes.
In a forest ecosystem, trees, shrubs and ferns are producers; deer, squirrels and caterpillars are herbivores; foxes, owls and wolves are predators; mushrooms are decomposers.
A forest food chain: acorns, squirrel, fox.
A forest food chain: leaves, caterpillar, bird, hawk.
In a pond ecosystem, algae and water plants are producers; insects, tadpoles and small fish are consumers; frogs, turtles, herons and large fish are predators.
A pond food chain: algae, insect larvae, small fish, frog, heron.
A pond food chain: algae, tadpole, fish, raccoon.
In an ocean ecosystem, phytoplankton and seaweed are producers; zooplankton, krill and small fish are consumers; sharks, seals, orcas and tuna are predators.
An ocean food chain: phytoplankton, krill, whale.
An ocean food chain: algae, small fish, tuna, shark.
Baleen whales filter krill, tiny shrimp-like animals, from the water.
In an arctic ecosystem, lichens, mosses and grasses are producers; caribou, lemmings and arctic hares are herbivores; arctic foxes, wolves, snowy owls and polar bears are predators.
Polar bears hunt seals, which eat fish, which eat smaller animals and plankton.
An arctic food chain: algae, small fish, seal, polar bear.
In a rain forest ecosystem, trees and vines are producers; monkeys, sloths, parrots, insects and frogs are consumers; jaguars and snakes are predators.
In a coral reef, algae living inside coral make food; fish, sea turtles, sea stars and sharks live there.
Parrotfish eat algae off coral; reef sharks eat fish.
In a wetland or marsh, cattails and grasses are producers; insects, frogs, ducks, fish and alligators are consumers.
In a salt marsh, crabs, snails and wading birds feed in the mud.
In a river, insects, fish, otters and bears depend on clean flowing water.
In soil, earthworms, bacteria, fungi and insects decompose dead material and recycle nutrients.
A rotting log is a habitat for fungi, insects, worms and salamanders, which decompose the wood.
Owls eat mice, voles, shrews and insects.
Hawks eat mice, rabbits, snakes and small birds.
Snakes eat mice, frogs, eggs and insects.
Frogs eat insects such as flies, crickets and mosquitoes.
Spiders eat insects.
Ladybugs eat aphids, which helps gardeners.
Bats eat insects such as mosquitoes and moths, or fruit and nectar.
Bears eat berries, fish, insects, nuts and small animals.
Raccoons eat fruit, insects, eggs, fish and garbage.
Foxes eat rabbits, mice, birds and berries.
Wolves hunt deer, elk, moose and caribou.
Lions hunt zebras, antelope and wildebeest.
Cheetahs hunt gazelles.
Sharks eat fish, seals and squid.
Seals eat fish and squid.
Penguins eat fish, krill and squid.
Pandas eat mostly bamboo.
Koalas eat eucalyptus leaves.
Caterpillars eat leaves; monarch caterpillars eat milkweed.
Deer eat grass, leaves, twigs, acorns and shrubs.
Rabbits eat grass, clover and vegetables.
Grasshoppers eat grass and leaves.
Squirrels eat nuts, seeds and acorns.
Cows and sheep eat grass and have stomachs with several chambers to digest it.
Hummingbirds drink nectar; bees collect nectar and pollen.
Earthworms eat soil and decaying plant material.
Vultures and hyenas eat dead animals.
Termites eat wood with help from microorganisms in their guts.
Mushrooms grow on dead logs and break down wood.
Barnacles attach to rocks, ships and whales.
Remoras attach to sharks to eat leftover food, which is commensalism.
Cattle egrets eat insects stirred up by grazing cattle, which is commensalism.
Tapeworms live in the intestines of animals, which is parasitism.
Fleas and ticks are parasites that feed on the blood of dogs, deer and people.
Mistletoe is a parasitic plant that takes water and nutrients from trees.
Clownfish live among sea anemone tentacles; the anemone protects the clownfish, and the clownfish cleans the anemone.
Ants protect acacia trees, and the trees give the ants food and shelter.
Cleaner fish eat parasites off larger fish, a mutualism.
Cows have bacteria in their stomachs that help digest grass, a mutualism.
Humans have helpful bacteria in their intestines that help digest food.
Pollinating insects and flowering plants depend on each other.
When a new species such as kudzu or zebra mussels invades, native species may lose food and space.

Rabbits introduced to Australia multiplied quickly because they had no predators.
Asian carp in rivers compete with native fish for food.
Pythons released in the Everglades eat many native animals.

Overfishing of cod reduced cod populations.

The dodo became extinct after humans arrived on its island.

Sea otters keep sea urchin populations in check, protecting kelp forests.
Beavers create wetlands that support fish, birds and amphibians.

Amphibians such as frogs are sensitive to pollution because they absorb water through their skin.
Frogs are indicator species: declining frog populations show environmental problems.
Lichens are sensitive to air pollution, so they indicate air quality.
Mayfly and stonefly larvae live only in clean streams, so they indicate good water quality.
Coral bleaching happens when water gets too warm and corals lose their algae.
Melting sea ice makes it harder for polar bears to hunt seals.
Warmer temperatures let some insects and diseases spread to new areas.
Some plants flower earlier in spring as climates warm.
