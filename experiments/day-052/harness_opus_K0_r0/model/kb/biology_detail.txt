# More biology detail
Ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis in the cell.
ATP is the molecule cells use to store and transfer energy.
Enzymes work best at certain temperatures and pH; too much heat can destroy them.
Enzymes in saliva and the stomach break down food molecules.
Glucose is the main sugar cells use for energy.
Starch is a carbohydrate plants use to store glucose.
Cellulose is a carbohydrate that makes up plant cell walls and is fiber in our diet.
Lipids, or fats and oils, store energy and make up cell membranes.
Proteins are made of amino acids.
DNA is made of nucleotides with four bases: adenine, thymine, guanine and cytosine.
The order of bases in DNA is a code for making proteins.
Each cell in a person's body has the same DNA, but different genes are turned on in different cells.
Cell specialization means cells develop different structures to do different jobs.
Xylem and phloem are vascular tissues in plants.
Plants have tissues such as epidermis to protect, xylem and phloem to transport, and mesophyll for photosynthesis.
Sexual reproduction in plants involves pollen and eggs; asexual reproduction includes runners, bulbs, tubers and cuttings.
Fungi reproduce with spores carried by wind and water.
Yeast reproduces by budding.
Hydra reproduce by budding.
Planaria and sea stars can regenerate lost body parts.
Bacteria can reproduce very quickly, dividing every 20 minutes in good conditions.
Some bacteria form spores that survive harsh conditions.
Bacteria help make cheese, yogurt, sauerkraut and pickles.
Bacteria in the roots of legumes fix nitrogen.
Some bacteria and fungi cause food to spoil.
Protists include algae, amoebas, paramecia and slime molds.
Malaria is caused by a protist carried by mosquitoes.
Plankton are tiny organisms that drift in water; phytoplankton make food, zooplankton eat it.
Diatoms are tiny algae with glass-like shells.
Lichens are made of a fungus and an alga living together.
Eutrophication happens when excess nutrients from fertilizers cause algae blooms that use up oxygen in water.
Dead zones in oceans have too little oxygen for fish because of algae blooms from fertilizer runoff.
Wetland plants filter pollutants from water.
Codominance occurs when both alleles are expressed, such as a red and white flower producing a spotted flower.
Incomplete dominance produces a blend, such as red and white flowers producing pink flowers.
Sex-linked traits such as color blindness are carried on the X chromosome and are more common in males.
A karyotype is a picture of a person's chromosomes.
Genetic testing can show whether a person carries certain genes.
DNA fingerprinting is used to identify people because each person's DNA is unique, except identical twins.
Biotechnology uses bacteria to make insulin for people with diabetes.
Gene therapy tries to fix faulty genes that cause disease.
Cloning produces an organism with the same DNA as its parent; Dolly the sheep was the first cloned mammal.
Vestigial structures, such as the human appendix and whale hip bones, show evolution from ancestors.
Embryos of fish, birds and mammals look similar in early stages, which suggests common ancestry.
Whales evolved from land mammals.
Horses evolved from small animals with several toes into large animals with one hoof.
Natural selection acts on individuals, but populations evolve.
Adaptive radiation is when one species evolves into many species adapted to different environments, such as Darwin's finches.
Fossils show that simple organisms appeared before complex ones.
Cyanobacteria were among the first organisms to produce oxygen by photosynthesis.
Homeostasis examples include sweating to cool down, shivering to warm up, and controlling blood sugar.
The kidneys help maintain homeostasis by balancing water and salts in the blood.
The nervous system and endocrine system both control and coordinate body functions.
The nervous system works fast using electrical signals; the endocrine system works more slowly using hormones.
Adrenal glands produce adrenaline in stressful situations, increasing heart rate.
The thyroid gland controls the rate of metabolism.
The pituitary gland controls growth and other glands.
Insulin from the pancreas lowers blood sugar.
Estrogen and testosterone are hormones that cause changes during puberty.
The placenta provides nutrients and oxygen from the mother to the developing baby.
Human pregnancy lasts about nine months.
Mammals feed their young with milk from mammary glands.
The appendix, tonsils and spleen are part of the lymphatic and immune systems.
The lymphatic system returns fluid to the blood and helps fight infection.
Antibodies are proteins made by white blood cells that attach to specific germs.
Immunity means the body can resist a disease, often after a vaccination or an infection.
Herd immunity happens when enough people are vaccinated to stop a disease from spreading.
Pathogens can be spread by contaminated water, so clean water prevents diseases like cholera.
Mosquitoes spread malaria, West Nile virus and Zika virus; ticks spread Lyme disease.
Rats and fleas spread the plague.
Food poisoning is caused by bacteria such as salmonella in undercooked food.
Cooking meat thoroughly kills harmful bacteria.
Diabetes is a disease in which the body cannot control the level of sugar in the blood.
Heart disease is linked to smoking, lack of exercise and diets high in fat.
Lung cancer is linked to smoking.
Skin cancer is linked to too much ultraviolet light from the Sun.
Scurvy is caused by lack of vitamin C; rickets is caused by lack of vitamin D.
Anemia can be caused by not enough iron in the diet.
Goiter is caused by lack of iodine.
Obesity increases the risk of diabetes and heart disease.
Malnutrition is caused by not eating enough of the right nutrients.
Animals that live in large groups can spread diseases more easily.
Plant diseases can be caused by fungi, bacteria and viruses.
Crop diversity helps prevent a single disease from wiping out a food supply.
The Irish potato famine was caused by a fungus-like disease that destroyed potato crops.
Bees, butterflies, bats, birds and wind pollinate plants.
Flowers pollinated by wind, such as grasses, are small and dull with lots of light pollen.
Flowers pollinated by insects are often brightly colored and scented with nectar.
Night-blooming flowers are often white and fragrant to attract moths and bats.
Some plants, such as peas, can self-pollinate.
Fruits that are sweet and colorful attract animals that eat them and spread their seeds.
Seeds that stick to fur have hooks or barbs.
Seeds carried by water are light and waterproof and can float.
A seed can remain dormant until conditions are right for germination.
The first structure to come out of a germinating seed is the root.
Monocots, such as grasses and corn, have one seed leaf; dicots, such as beans, have two seed leaves.
Leaves have veins that carry water and food.
The upper side of a leaf usually gets the most sunlight.
Guard cells control the opening and closing of stomata to balance gas exchange and water loss.
Stomata usually close at night and during hot dry weather to save water.
Plants growing in shade often have larger, thinner leaves.
Plants transport water upward partly because of transpiration pulling water from the leaves.
Celery placed in colored water shows how water travels up through the xylem of the stem.
Plants make more food in summer when there is more sunlight.
Photosynthesis stops when there is no light; respiration continues day and night.
Plants are both producers of oxygen and users of oxygen.
Plants give off more oxygen than they use during the day.
Aquatic plants release oxygen bubbles in bright light.
Elodea is a water plant often used to observe photosynthesis.
