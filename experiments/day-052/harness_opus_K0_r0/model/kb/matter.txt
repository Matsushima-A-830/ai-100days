# Matter: properties, states, changes, atoms, elements, compounds, mixtures, reactions, acids and bases
Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space.
Mass is the amount of matter in an object.
Volume is the amount of space an object takes up.
Density is the mass of an object divided by its volume; density equals mass divided by volume.
An object that is less dense than water floats; an object that is more dense than water sinks.
The density of water is 1 gram per cubic centimeter.
Oil floats on water because oil is less dense than water.
Wood floats on water because it is less dense; a rock or a metal nail sinks because it is denser.
Cutting an object in half does not change its density.
Physical properties can be observed or measured without changing what a substance is: color, shape, size, mass, volume, density, texture, hardness, odor, melting point, boiling point, magnetism, conductivity and solubility.
Chemical properties describe how a substance changes into new substances, such as flammability and the ability to rust or react with acid.
Flammability is the ability of a substance to burn.
The states of matter are solid, liquid and gas; plasma is a fourth state.
A solid has a definite shape and a definite volume.
A liquid has a definite volume but no definite shape; it takes the shape of its container.
A gas has no definite shape and no definite volume; it spreads out to fill its container.
In a solid, particles are packed tightly and vibrate in place.
In a liquid, particles are close together but can slide past each other.
In a gas, particles are far apart and move quickly and freely.
Gases can be compressed because their particles are far apart.
Particles of matter are always moving; they move faster when the temperature is higher.
Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy, or motion, of the particles in a substance.
Adding heat makes particles move faster; removing heat makes them move slower.
A change of state is a physical change.
Melting is the change from a solid to a liquid when heat is added.
Freezing is the change from a liquid to a solid when heat is removed.
Evaporation and boiling are changes from a liquid to a gas.
Boiling happens throughout a liquid at the boiling point; evaporation happens only at the surface and at any temperature.
Condensation is the change from a gas to a liquid when heat is removed.
Sublimation is the change from a solid directly to a gas, like dry ice, frozen carbon dioxide.
Deposition is the change from a gas directly to a solid, like frost forming.
Water freezes and ice melts at 0 degrees Celsius, or 32 degrees Fahrenheit.
Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius, or 212 degrees Fahrenheit, at sea level.
The temperature of ice water stays at 0 degrees Celsius until all the ice melts.
During a change of state the temperature stays the same while heat energy is added or removed.
Ice melts faster in a warm place than in a cold place.
Ice cubes melt into liquid water when heat is added, a physical change.
Water is the only substance that is commonly found as a solid, liquid and gas on Earth.
When water freezes, it expands and becomes less dense, so ice floats.
Most substances contract when they cool and expand when they are heated.
Thermal expansion is the increase in size of a material when it is heated.
Bridges and sidewalks have gaps so they can expand in hot weather without cracking.
A liquid thermometer works because the liquid expands as it gets warmer and rises in the tube.
A balloon gets bigger when warmed and smaller when cooled because the gas inside expands and contracts.
A physical change changes the size, shape or state of matter but does not make a new substance.
Examples of physical changes are cutting, tearing, folding, breaking glass, crushing, melting, freezing, boiling, evaporating and dissolving.
Dissolving sugar or salt in water is a physical change because the sugar or salt can be recovered by evaporating the water.
A chemical change, or chemical reaction, produces one or more new substances with new properties.
Examples of chemical changes are burning, rusting, cooking, baking, digesting food, rotting, and fruit ripening.
Signs of a chemical change include a color change, gas bubbles, a new smell, light, heat, temperature change and forming a precipitate.
Burning wood is a chemical change that produces ash, smoke, carbon dioxide, heat and light.
Rusting is a chemical change in which iron reacts with oxygen and water to form iron oxide.
Iron rusts faster in moist air; paint or oil protects iron from rusting.
Baking a cake is a chemical change.
Milk turning sour and bread molding are chemical changes.
Mixing baking soda and vinegar is a chemical change that produces carbon dioxide gas bubbles.
A tarnished silver spoon has undergone a chemical change.
Fireworks and burning candles involve chemical changes that give off light and heat.
Photosynthesis and digestion are chemical changes.
An atom is the smallest particle of an element that has the properties of that element.
Atoms are made of protons, neutrons and electrons.
Protons have a positive charge, electrons have a negative charge, and neutrons have no charge.
Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus at the center of the atom; electrons move around the nucleus.
Most of the mass of an atom is in its nucleus.
Electrons have much less mass than protons and neutrons.
The number of protons in an atom is its atomic number, and it identifies the element.
Atoms of the same element have the same number of protons.
Isotopes are atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
An atom is neutral when it has equal numbers of protons and electrons.
An ion is an atom that has gained or lost electrons and has a charge.
Electrons are involved in chemical bonds.
A covalent bond forms when atoms share electrons.
An ionic bond forms when electrons transfer from one atom to another, as in sodium chloride.
An oxygen molecule is made of two oxygen atoms that share two pairs of electrons in a double covalent bond.
An element is a pure substance made of only one kind of atom.
There are about 118 known elements.
Elements are arranged in the periodic table by atomic number.
Elements in the same column, or group, of the periodic table have similar properties.
Metals are on the left side of the periodic table; nonmetals are on the right side.
Metals are shiny, malleable, ductile, and good conductors of heat and electricity.
Malleable means a material can be hammered into thin sheets; ductile means it can be drawn into wires.
Nonmetals are usually dull, brittle and poor conductors.
Metalloids such as silicon have properties of both metals and nonmetals.
Noble gases such as helium, neon and argon are very unreactive.
Helium is less dense than air, so helium balloons float.
Hydrogen is the lightest and most common element in the universe.
Oxygen is the most abundant element in Earth's crust and is needed for burning and for respiration.
Carbon is found in all living things.
Common elements and symbols: H hydrogen, He helium, C carbon, N nitrogen, O oxygen, Na sodium, Cl chlorine, Fe iron, Cu copper, Au gold, Ag silver, Al aluminum, Ca calcium, K potassium.
A compound is a substance made of two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio.
Water, H2O, is a compound made of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.
Carbon dioxide, CO2, is a compound of one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms.
Table salt, sodium chloride NaCl, is a compound of sodium and chlorine.
A compound has different properties from the elements that form it; sodium is a reactive metal and chlorine is a poisonous gas, but together they form table salt.
A molecule is two or more atoms joined by chemical bonds.
A chemical formula shows the elements and number of atoms in a compound.
A mixture is two or more substances physically combined; each keeps its own properties.
Mixtures can be separated by physical means such as filtering, evaporation, magnets, sorting and sieving.
Examples of mixtures are salad, trail mix, sand and water, salt water, air, soil and granite.
Salt water is a mixture that can be separated by evaporating the water, leaving the salt.
A magnet can separate iron filings from sand.
Filtering separates solids from liquids, such as sand from water.
A heterogeneous mixture has parts you can see, such as salad or sand and gravel.
A homogeneous mixture, or solution, is the same throughout, such as salt water or sugar water.
A solution is a mixture in which one substance, the solute, dissolves in another, the solvent.
Water is called the universal solvent because it dissolves many substances.
Sugar dissolves faster in hot water than in cold water.
Stirring, heating and crushing make a solid dissolve faster.
Solubility is the amount of a substance that can dissolve in a solvent.
Sand does not dissolve in water; it is insoluble.
A suspension is a mixture in which particles settle out over time, such as muddy water.
Distillation separates liquids by boiling and condensing them.
Chromatography separates the colors in ink.
The law of conservation of mass states that matter is not created or destroyed in a chemical reaction; total mass stays the same.
In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged to form new substances, but the number of each kind of atom stays the same.
In a closed container, the mass before and after a reaction is the same.
In a chemical equation, the reactants are on the left and the products are on the right.
Combustion, or burning, is a chemical reaction of a fuel with oxygen that releases heat and light.
Fire needs fuel, oxygen and heat; removing any one puts out the fire.
Covering a fire or a candle removes oxygen and puts it out.
Exothermic reactions release heat; endothermic reactions absorb heat and feel cold.
A catalyst speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up.
Reactions happen faster at higher temperatures, with smaller pieces, and with higher concentrations.
Acids taste sour, turn blue litmus paper red, and have a pH less than 7.
Bases taste bitter, feel slippery, turn red litmus paper blue, and have a pH greater than 7.
A pH of 7 is neutral; pure water has a pH of 7.
The pH scale goes from 0 to 14; lower numbers are more acidic.
Lemon juice, vinegar and stomach acid are acids.
Baking soda, soap, ammonia and bleach are bases.
Mixing an acid and a base produces water and a salt in a neutralization reaction.
Antacids are bases that neutralize stomach acid.
Litmus paper and other indicators show whether a substance is an acid or a base.
Polymers are long chains of repeating molecules, such as plastics, rubber and proteins.
Plastic is made from petroleum, or oil.
Alloys are mixtures of metals, such as steel, brass and bronze.
Steel is an alloy of iron and carbon; it is stronger than iron.
Bronze is made of copper and tin; brass is made of copper and zinc.
Gold and platinum do not react easily, so they do not rust or tarnish.
Aluminum is a light metal that does not rust, used for cans, foil and airplanes.
Copper is used for electrical wires because it conducts electricity well.
Diamond is the hardest natural substance.
Glass is made by melting sand.
Water is made of hydrogen and oxygen.
Ice, liquid water and water vapor are the same substance in different states.
Mass is measured in grams or kilograms with a balance.
Volume of a liquid is measured in milliliters or liters with a graduated cylinder.
The volume of an irregular solid can be found by water displacement in a graduated cylinder.
The volume of a rectangular box equals length times width times height.
When a solid melts, its mass stays the same.
When salt dissolves in water, the mass of the solution equals the mass of the salt plus the mass of the water.
When ice melts, the mass of the water is the same as the mass of the ice.
Gas particles exert pressure by colliding with the walls of their container.
Heating a gas in a closed container increases its pressure.
Smells spread through a room because gas particles move and spread out, called diffusion.
Food coloring spreads faster in hot water than in cold water because the particles move faster.
Plasma is a very hot state of matter made of charged particles, found in stars and lightning.
A property such as magnetism can be used to separate iron from other materials.
Texture describes how something feels, such as rough or smooth.
A substance that is brittle breaks easily, like glass.
Flexibility means a material can bend without breaking, like rubber.
Conductivity is how well a material lets heat or electricity flow.
Particle size can be used to separate mixtures using a screen or sieve.
Pure substances include elements and compounds; they have a fixed composition.
Neutral atoms have the same number of electrons as protons.
Elements combine to form all the different substances in the world.
Matter can change form but the total amount of matter stays the same.
Burning paper changes it into ash and gases, but the total mass of the products equals the mass of the paper and the oxygen used.
