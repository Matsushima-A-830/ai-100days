# More chemistry and physics detail
The periodic table arranges elements in rows called periods and columns called groups or families.
Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons and similar chemical properties.
Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost shell of an atom; they determine how an atom bonds.
Alkali metals such as sodium and potassium in group 1 are very reactive and react strongly with water.
Alkaline earth metals such as calcium and magnesium are in group 2.
Halogens such as fluorine and chlorine in group 17 are very reactive nonmetals.
Noble gases such as helium, neon and argon in group 18 have full outer electron shells and rarely react.
Neon is used in glowing signs.
Transition metals such as iron, copper, gold and silver are in the middle of the periodic table.
Metals tend to lose electrons; nonmetals tend to gain electrons.
Atoms bond to become more stable by filling their outer electron shells.
The atomic mass is about equal to the number of protons plus neutrons.
The number of neutrons equals the mass number minus the atomic number.
Hydrogen has one proton; helium has two protons; carbon has six protons; oxygen has eight protons.
A water molecule has two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.
Glucose, C6H12O6, is a sugar made of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.
Carbon dioxide has one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms.
The formula for table salt is NaCl.
Ozone is a molecule of three oxygen atoms, O3.
Oxygen gas is made of molecules of two oxygen atoms, O2.
The photosynthesis equation: carbon dioxide plus water with light energy produces glucose and oxygen.
The respiration equation: glucose plus oxygen produces carbon dioxide, water and energy.
In a balanced chemical equation, the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides.
Reactants are the starting substances in a reaction; products are the new substances formed.
A precipitate is a solid that forms from two liquids in a chemical reaction.
Gas bubbles, color change, light, heat, odor and a precipitate are evidence of a chemical reaction.
Temperature change alone may happen in either physical or chemical changes.
Burning methane produces carbon dioxide and water.
When hydrogen burns it combines with oxygen to form water.
Electrolysis uses electricity to split water into hydrogen and oxygen gases.
Mass is conserved when ice melts, when sugar dissolves and when a reaction occurs in a closed container.
If a gas escapes from an open container during a reaction, the measured mass decreases.
Concentration is the amount of solute dissolved in a given amount of solvent.
A saturated solution cannot dissolve any more solute at that temperature.
More solid solute can dissolve in hot water than in cold water.
Gases such as carbon dioxide dissolve better in cold water; warm soda goes flat faster.
Adding salt to water raises its boiling point and lowers its freezing point.
Pure substances have definite melting and boiling points that can be used to identify them.
Density can be used to identify a substance because each substance has its own density.
The density of a substance does not depend on the size of the sample.
A larger piece of the same material has more mass and more volume but the same density.
Liquids of different densities form layers, with the densest at the bottom, such as honey, water and oil.
Gold is very dense; a small gold bar has a large mass.
Mercury is a metal that is liquid at room temperature.
Bromine is a nonmetal that is liquid at room temperature.
Carbon forms many compounds and is the basis of organic molecules in living things.
Hydrocarbons such as methane, propane and octane in gasoline are made of hydrogen and carbon.
Radioactive elements such as uranium and radium give off radiation as their nuclei decay.
Nuclear fission splits a large nucleus into smaller ones and releases energy.
Nuclear fusion joins small nuclei into a larger one and releases energy, as in the Sun.
Kinetic energy depends on mass and speed: doubling the speed of an object makes its kinetic energy four times greater.
Potential energy due to gravity equals mass times gravity times height.
Work equals force times distance, measured in joules.
Efficiency is the amount of useful energy output compared to the energy input.
No machine is 100 percent efficient because some energy is always lost as heat due to friction.
A perpetual motion machine is impossible because energy is lost to friction.
Thermal energy flows from a hot cup of cocoa to the cooler air in a room until they are the same temperature.
When hot and cold water are mixed, the final temperature is between the two starting temperatures.
Specific heat is the amount of energy needed to raise the temperature of a substance; water has a high specific heat.
Sand heats up faster than water in the sunlight because sand has a lower specific heat.
Metals heat up quickly because they have low specific heat and are good conductors.
Absolute zero is the coldest possible temperature, when particles have the least motion.
The kelvin scale starts at absolute zero; 0 degrees Celsius equals 273 kelvins.
To convert Celsius to Fahrenheit, multiply by 9/5 and add 32.
Pressure in a liquid increases with depth; divers feel more pressure deeper underwater.
Submarines dive by filling tanks with water and rise by pumping the water out.
Airplanes fly because wings are shaped to create lift as air moves over them.
The four forces on an airplane are lift, weight, thrust and drag.
Rockets carry their own oxygen because there is no air in space.
A force acting at a distance includes gravity, magnetic force and electric force.
Contact forces include friction, tension, normal force and applied force.
Tension is the pulling force in a rope or string.
The normal force is the upward push of a surface on an object resting on it.
An object resting on a table has balanced forces: gravity pulls down and the table pushes up.
When forces are balanced, a moving object keeps moving at a constant speed in a straight line.
Two forces in the same direction add together; forces in opposite directions subtract.
A heavier ball and a lighter ball dropped together from the same height land at about the same time when air resistance is small.
Terminal velocity is reached when air resistance equals the weight of a falling object.
Centripetal force keeps an object moving in a circle; for planets it is gravity.
A pendulum's swing time, its period, depends on its length, not its mass; a longer pendulum swings more slowly.
Mass does not change the period of a pendulum.
Springs stretch farther when more force or weight is applied.
Elastic materials return to their original shape after being stretched.
Magnets lose strength when heated or dropped.
Earth's magnetic north pole is near the geographic North Pole and slowly moves over time.
The strength of a magnetic or electric force decreases as distance increases.
An electric current in a wire creates a magnetic field around the wire, which can move a compass needle.
Transformers change the voltage of electricity in power lines.
Alternating current changes direction many times a second; batteries produce direct current.
Electrical energy is converted to light, heat, sound and motion in household devices.
Electric power in watts equals voltage times current.
A 100-watt bulb uses more energy per second than a 60-watt bulb.
Thicker wires have less resistance; longer wires have more resistance.
Light bulbs in parallel stay bright; adding bulbs in series makes each bulb dimmer.
Light is both a wave and a stream of particles called photons.
Light with more energy, such as ultraviolet, has a higher frequency and shorter wavelength.
Visible light is only a small part of the electromagnetic spectrum.
Polarized sunglasses reduce glare by blocking light waves vibrating in certain directions.
Concave mirrors, like those in flashlights and telescopes, focus light; convex mirrors, like car side mirrors, show a wider view.
Light slows down when it enters water or glass, which causes refraction.
Diamonds sparkle because they refract and reflect light strongly.
Color filters let only certain colors of light pass through; a red filter lets red light through.
Mixing red, green and blue light makes white light.
Our eyes have cone cells that detect color and rod cells that work in dim light.
The Doppler effect is the change in pitch of a sound as the source moves toward or away from you, like a passing siren.
Sound waves spread out and get weaker as they travel farther from the source.
Sound travels faster in warm air than in cold air.
Musical instruments make sound by vibrating strings, air columns, or surfaces such as drumheads.
A tuning fork vibrates at one frequency and makes one pitch.
Resonance happens when an object vibrates at its natural frequency because of another vibration.
Earthquake waves and ocean waves carry energy over long distances.
The speed of a wave equals its frequency times its wavelength.
