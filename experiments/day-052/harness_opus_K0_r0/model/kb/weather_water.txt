# Water cycle, atmosphere, weather, climate, oceans
The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between Earth's surface and the atmosphere.
The main processes of the water cycle are evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection or runoff.
The Sun provides the energy that drives the water cycle.
Energy from the Sun causes water to evaporate from oceans, lakes and rivers.
Evaporation is the change of liquid water into water vapor, a gas.
Water evaporates faster when it is warm, windy, and when the surface area is larger.
A puddle dries up because the water evaporates into the air.
Wet clothes dry on a clothesline because the water evaporates.
Condensation is the change of water vapor into liquid water when it cools.
Clouds form when water vapor in the air cools and condenses into tiny water droplets or ice crystals.
Dew forms when water vapor condenses on cool surfaces such as grass at night.
Water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass because water vapor in the air condenses on the cold surface.
Fog is a cloud that forms near the ground.
Frost forms when water vapor turns directly into ice on cold surfaces.
Precipitation is water that falls from clouds to Earth as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
Precipitation happens when water droplets in clouds combine and become too heavy to stay in the air.
Rain is liquid precipitation; snow forms when water vapor freezes into ice crystals.
Sleet is rain that freezes into ice pellets as it falls through cold air.
Hail forms when strong updrafts in thunderstorms carry raindrops up into freezing air again and again, building layers of ice.
Runoff is precipitation that flows over the ground into streams, rivers, lakes and oceans.
Some precipitation soaks into the ground, called infiltration, and becomes groundwater.
Plants release water vapor into the air through their leaves by transpiration.
Transpiration from plants is part of the water cycle.
Most of Earth's water, about 97 percent, is salt water in the oceans.
Only about 3 percent of Earth's water is fresh water, and most of that is frozen in glaciers and ice caps.
Very little of Earth's water is fresh liquid water available in lakes, rivers and groundwater.
Oceans cover about 70 percent, or nearly three fourths, of Earth's surface.
The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest ocean.
Ocean water is salty because rivers carry dissolved salts and minerals from rocks into the ocean.
When ocean water evaporates, the salt is left behind, so rain is fresh water.
A watershed is an area of land that drains into a river or lake.
The atmosphere is the layer of gases that surrounds Earth.
Earth's atmosphere is about 78 percent nitrogen and 21 percent oxygen, with small amounts of argon, carbon dioxide and water vapor.
Nitrogen is the most common gas in Earth's atmosphere.
The layers of the atmosphere are the troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere and thermosphere.
The troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere, where almost all weather occurs.
The stratosphere contains the ozone layer.
The ozone layer absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation from the Sun.
Chemicals called CFCs damaged the ozone layer.
Air pressure is the force of the weight of air pushing on a surface.
Air pressure decreases as altitude increases; there is less air high on a mountain.
Air temperature generally decreases as altitude increases in the troposphere.
A barometer measures air pressure.
Falling air pressure usually means stormy or rainy weather is coming.
Rising air pressure usually means clear, fair weather is coming.
High pressure systems usually bring clear, dry weather; low pressure systems bring clouds and precipitation.
A thermometer measures temperature.
An anemometer measures wind speed.
A wind vane, or weather vane, shows wind direction.
A rain gauge measures the amount of rainfall.
A hygrometer or psychrometer measures humidity.
Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a certain place and time, including temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity and air pressure.
Climate is the average weather of a region over a long period of time, many years.
Weather changes from day to day, but climate describes long-term patterns.
Humidity is the amount of water vapor in the air.
Relative humidity compares the amount of water vapor in the air to the most it can hold at that temperature.
Warm air can hold more water vapor than cold air.
The dew point is the temperature at which water vapor condenses.
Wind is the movement of air caused by differences in air pressure.
Wind is caused by the uneven heating of Earth's surface by the Sun.
Air moves from areas of high pressure to areas of low pressure, causing wind.
Warm air is less dense and rises; cool air is denser and sinks.
A sea breeze blows from the ocean to the land during the day because land heats faster than water.
A land breeze blows from the land to the ocean at night because land cools faster than water.
Land heats up and cools down faster than water.
Water has a high specific heat, so it heats up and cools down slowly.
Large bodies of water keep nearby coastal areas cooler in summer and warmer in winter.
Coastal cities have milder climates than inland cities because of the ocean.
An air mass is a large body of air with similar temperature and humidity throughout.
Air masses that form over oceans are moist; air masses that form over land are dry.
Air masses that form near the equator are warm; air masses that form near the poles are cold.
A front is the boundary between two air masses.
A cold front forms when cold air pushes under warm air; it often brings thunderstorms and then cooler weather.
A warm front forms when warm air moves over cold air; it often brings steady rain and then warmer weather.
A stationary front happens when two air masses meet and neither moves.
On weather maps, a cold front is shown with blue triangles and a warm front with red half circles.
Meteorologists are scientists who study weather and make weather forecasts.
Meteorologists use weather maps, satellites, radar, weather balloons and computer models to forecast weather.
Doppler radar shows where precipitation is falling and how storms move.
Cirrus clouds are thin, wispy, feathery clouds high in the sky made of ice crystals; they often mean fair weather or a change coming.
Cumulus clouds are puffy white clouds with flat bottoms that usually mean fair weather.
Cumulonimbus clouds are tall, dark thunderhead clouds that bring thunderstorms, lightning, heavy rain and hail.
Stratus clouds are flat, gray layers that cover the sky and may bring drizzle.
Nimbostratus clouds bring steady rain or snow.
Thunderstorms form when warm, moist air rises quickly.
Lightning is a giant spark of static electricity in a storm.
Thunder is the sound caused by lightning rapidly heating and expanding the air.
We see lightning before we hear thunder because light travels faster than sound.
A tornado is a violently rotating column of air extending from a thunderstorm to the ground.
Tornadoes have the fastest winds of any storm but are small and short-lived.
Many tornadoes occur in the central United States, called Tornado Alley, in spring.
A hurricane is a large, powerful tropical storm with winds over 119 kilometers per hour that forms over warm ocean water.
Hurricanes get their energy from warm ocean water and weaken when they move over land or cold water.
The eye of a hurricane is a calm area in its center.
Hurricanes cause damage from strong winds, heavy rain, flooding and storm surge.
A blizzard is a snowstorm with strong winds and low visibility.
A drought is a long period with little or no rain.
Droughts can cause crops to fail, water shortages and wildfires.
Floods happen when heavy rain or melting snow causes water to overflow onto land.
Weather forecasts help people prepare for storms and stay safe.
During a thunderstorm, people should go indoors and stay away from tall objects, water and metal.
During a tornado warning, people should go to a basement or an inner room without windows.
Climate depends on latitude, altitude, distance from the ocean, ocean currents and prevailing winds.
Places closer to the equator are generally warmer; places closer to the poles are colder.
Places at higher altitude, such as mountain tops, are colder.
Tropical climates near the equator are hot and often rainy all year.
Polar climates are very cold with little precipitation.
Temperate climates have four seasons: winter, spring, summer and fall.
Deserts receive very little rainfall, less than 25 centimeters a year.
Deserts can be very hot in the day and cold at night because dry air and clear skies let heat escape.
Mountains cause a rain shadow: the side facing the wind is wet and the other side is dry.
As moist air rises over a mountain, it cools and drops rain on the windward side.
Ocean currents carry warm water from the equator toward the poles and cold water toward the equator.
The Gulf Stream is a warm ocean current that keeps western Europe milder than other places at the same latitude.
Surface ocean currents are driven mainly by wind.
Deep ocean currents are caused by differences in water temperature and salinity, or density.
Cold, salty water is dense and sinks.
El Nino is a change in Pacific Ocean temperatures that affects weather around the world.
The Coriolis effect, caused by Earth's rotation, curves the paths of winds and ocean currents.
Global winds, such as the trade winds and westerlies, are caused by uneven heating and Earth's rotation.
In the United States, weather usually moves from west to east because of the prevailing westerlies.
The jet stream is a fast, high-altitude wind that steers storms.
Ocean waves are mostly caused by wind blowing over the water.
The greenhouse effect is the trapping of heat by gases such as carbon dioxide, methane and water vapor in the atmosphere.
Greenhouse gases let sunlight in but absorb and trap heat radiated from Earth's surface.
Without the greenhouse effect, Earth would be too cold for most life.
Increasing carbon dioxide in the atmosphere from burning fossil fuels is causing global warming.
Global warming, or climate change, is the gradual increase of Earth's average temperature.
Climate change causes glaciers and polar ice to melt and sea level to rise.
Rising sea levels can flood coastal areas.
Cutting down forests increases carbon dioxide in the air because trees absorb carbon dioxide.
Ice ages were long periods when Earth's climate was much colder and glaciers covered large areas.
Ice cores, tree rings and fossils give evidence of past climates.
Tree rings that are wide show years with good growing conditions such as plenty of rain; narrow rings show dry years.
Water expands when it freezes, so ice is less dense than liquid water and floats.
Because ice floats, lakes freeze from the top down, and fish can survive in the water below.
Water is the only common substance found naturally on Earth as a solid, liquid and gas.
Snow melting in spring adds water to rivers and can cause floods.
The amount of water on Earth stays about the same because it is recycled in the water cycle.
Clouds are made of tiny water droplets or ice crystals, not water vapor.
Air has mass and takes up space.
Air is a mixture of gases.
Warm air rises because it is less dense than the cooler air around it, which is why hot air balloons rise.
Convection currents in the atmosphere move heat around Earth.
Sunlight heats land, water and air unevenly.
Dark surfaces such as asphalt and soil absorb more sunlight and heat up more than light surfaces such as snow.
Snow and ice reflect most sunlight.
Cities are often warmer than the countryside because pavement and buildings absorb heat.
A weather station records temperature, air pressure, humidity, wind speed, wind direction and precipitation.
Collecting weather data every day for many years shows the climate of a place.
In the Northern Hemisphere, July is usually warmer than January.
Severe weather includes thunderstorms, tornadoes, hurricanes, blizzards and floods.
Lightning rods protect buildings by carrying electric charge safely to the ground.
Rainbows form when sunlight is refracted and reflected by raindrops, separating the light into colors.
A rainbow is seen when the Sun is behind you and rain is in front of you.
The sky looks blue because air molecules scatter blue light from the Sun more than other colors.
Sunsets look red and orange because sunlight passes through more atmosphere, which scatters away the blue light.
Smog is air pollution made of smoke, fog and chemicals, often in cities.
Acid rain forms when sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from burning fossil fuels mix with water in the air.
Acid rain harms lakes, forests, fish and buildings.
