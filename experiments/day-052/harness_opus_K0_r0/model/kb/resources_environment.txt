# Natural resources, energy sources, pollution, conservation, human impact
A natural resource is a material from nature that people use, such as water, air, soil, minerals, plants, animals and fossil fuels.
Renewable resources can be replaced naturally in a short time, such as sunlight, wind, water, trees, plants and animals.
Nonrenewable resources cannot be replaced in a human lifetime and will run out, such as coal, oil, natural gas, minerals and uranium.
Renewable energy sources include solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, tidal and biomass energy.
Solar energy from the Sun is a renewable energy source.
Wind energy is renewable and does not produce air pollution.
Wind turbines use the wind to generate electricity.
Solar panels change sunlight into electricity without producing air pollution.
Solar energy is not available at night and is less available on cloudy days.
Wind energy only works when the wind is blowing.
Hydroelectric power uses flowing water in rivers and dams to turn turbines and produce electricity.
Dams can block fish migration and flood habitats.
Geothermal energy uses heat from inside Earth.
Tidal energy uses the movement of ocean tides.
Biomass energy comes from burning plant material, wood, or animal wastes, or from making fuels like ethanol from corn.
Trees are a renewable resource if new trees are planted to replace those that are cut.
Fossil fuels are coal, oil, petroleum and natural gas.
Fossil fuels formed over millions of years from the remains of ancient plants and animals buried under layers of sediment.
Coal formed from the remains of plants that lived in ancient swamps.
Oil and natural gas formed from the remains of tiny ocean organisms.
Fossil fuels are nonrenewable because they take millions of years to form.
Gasoline and diesel fuel are made from oil, or petroleum.
Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide, which contributes to global warming and climate change.
Burning fossil fuels causes air pollution, smog and acid rain.
Most electricity in the world is produced by burning fossil fuels.
Nuclear energy does not produce air pollution but produces radioactive waste that is dangerous for thousands of years.
Uranium used in nuclear power plants is a nonrenewable resource.
Energy stored in fossil fuels originally came from the Sun, captured by ancient plants through photosynthesis.
Pollution is anything harmful added to the environment.
Air pollution comes from burning fuels in cars, factories and power plants.
Water pollution comes from sewage, factory wastes, oil spills, pesticides, fertilizers and trash.
Land pollution comes from trash, landfills and chemicals.
Runoff from farms carries fertilizers and pesticides into rivers and lakes.
Plastic trash in the ocean harms sea turtles, birds and fish.
Littering pollutes land and water.
Landfills are places where trash is buried.
Conservation is the careful use and protection of natural resources.
The three Rs of conservation are reduce, reuse and recycle.
Reducing means using less of something, such as using less water, less electricity and less packaging.
Reusing means using items again, such as refilling water bottles and using cloth bags.
Recycling means processing used materials such as paper, glass, aluminum and plastic to make new products.
Recycling aluminum cans saves energy and reduces mining.
Recycling paper saves trees.
Recycling reduces the amount of trash in landfills and conserves natural resources.
Composting turns food scraps and yard waste into fertilizer for soil.
Turning off lights and electronics when not in use saves energy.
Using energy-efficient appliances and light bulbs conserves electricity.
Walking, riding a bike, carpooling and using public transportation save fuel and reduce air pollution.
Taking shorter showers and turning off the faucet while brushing teeth conserve water.
Fixing leaky faucets conserves water.
Insulating homes reduces the energy needed for heating and cooling.
Electric and hybrid cars use less gasoline and produce less pollution.
Deforestation is the cutting down of forests; it destroys habitats, increases erosion and adds carbon dioxide to the air.
Planting trees helps reduce carbon dioxide, prevents erosion and provides habitats.
Urban development and building roads and cities destroy natural habitats.
Wetlands are being drained for farms and buildings, which harms wildlife and increases flooding.
Overhunting and overfishing can cause species to become endangered or extinct.
Protecting habitats is the best way to protect endangered species.
National parks and wildlife refuges protect natural areas and wildlife.
Clean water is a limited resource that must be conserved.
Water treatment plants clean water to make it safe to drink.
Sewage treatment plants clean wastewater before it returns to rivers.
Many communities get drinking water from reservoirs, rivers, lakes and wells.
Desalination removes salt from ocean water to make fresh water, but it uses a lot of energy.
Soil is a natural resource needed to grow food.
Farmers conserve soil by contour plowing, terracing, crop rotation, planting cover crops and windbreaks.
Crop rotation means planting different crops in a field each year to keep the soil healthy.
Organic farming does not use synthetic pesticides or fertilizers.
Pesticides kill insect pests but can also harm helpful insects, birds and water supplies.
Integrated pest management uses natural predators, such as ladybugs eating aphids, to control pests.
Minerals and metals are mined from Earth; mining can damage land and pollute water.
Reclamation restores land after mining.
Ozone depletion was caused by CFC chemicals once used in refrigerators and spray cans.
Greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane, water vapor and nitrous oxide.
Methane is released by cattle, landfills and natural gas leaks.
Global warming is causing glaciers to melt, sea level to rise, and changes in habitats and weather patterns.
Reducing the use of fossil fuels reduces greenhouse gases.
People can reduce their carbon footprint by using less energy and driving less.
Catalytic converters on cars reduce air pollution.
Scrubbers on smokestacks remove pollutants from factory smoke.
Laws such as the Clean Air Act and Clean Water Act limit pollution.
Sustainable use of resources meets present needs without harming future generations.
Biodegradable materials can be broken down by decomposers, such as paper and food scraps.
Plastic is not biodegradable and lasts for hundreds of years.
Styrofoam and plastic bags take a very long time to break down.
Glass and aluminum can be recycled over and over.
Hazardous wastes such as batteries, paint and motor oil must be disposed of properly.
Noise pollution and light pollution can disturb people and animals.
Thermal pollution happens when warm water from power plants is released into rivers, lowering oxygen for fish.
Population growth increases the use of natural resources.
Natural resources are used to make products: trees for paper and lumber, oil for plastic and gasoline, sand for glass, and ores for metals.
Cotton and wool are natural fibers; nylon and polyester are synthetic fibers made from petroleum.
Wood comes from trees, a renewable resource.
Advantages of solar and wind energy are that they are renewable and do not pollute the air.
A disadvantage of fossil fuels is that they pollute the air and will eventually run out.
An advantage of fossil fuels is that they are easy to transport and store and produce a lot of energy.
Human activities such as farming, building and burning fuel change the environment.
People can help the environment by planting trees, recycling, conserving water and energy, and not littering.
